01 THE PROBLEM

A single run can
tell the wrong story.

A stochastic simulation can make a weak configuration look promising—or hide a good one behind noise. Repeating every candidate equally is expensive.

Aster explores a more selective approach: run evaluations in parallel, compare candidates under shared scenarios, and spend additional computation on decisions that remain uncertain.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

At a fixed worker count, can adaptive replication reach a predefined quality target with less time and compute?

02 ARCHITECTURE

Small enough to inspect.
Built around the experiment.

Read the design ↗

The current library connects a frozen candidate pool to local worker processes. Every result retains its seed, timing, and failure information.

    Candidate pool

    sample_configs()
  2. 02 / ALLOCATE

    Study + scheduler

    Assign replications and make pruning decisions.

    Study.optimize()
  3. 03 / EXECUTE

    Local workers

    Run independent objective evaluations in parallel.

    ProcessPoolExecutor
  4. 04 / INSPECT

    Results + audit

    Export observations; audit a locked configuration.

    result.save() · audit()
NEXT MILESTONE

Separate the coordinator from execution, then add persistent state and recovery. Simulated worker fleets are a proposed extension—not an implemented scaling result.

01

Repeatable inputs

Stable seeds separate training randomness, shared market scenarios, and independent audit evaluations.

02

Visible decisions

Paired comparisons record the observed disadvantage, standard error, and decision to continue or prune.

03

Explicit limits

Pruning is a heuristic. Repeated confidence checks do not establish a statistical quality guarantee.

DEVELOPMENT CHECK · SEPT 16, 2026

A small, reproducible start.

A saved synthetic run compares full-budget evaluation with adaptive replication on the same 16 configurations. Both select the same candidate in this run.

This is a functional smoke test. The objective is cheap, process startup dominates timing, and one seed does not establish performance or quality preservation.

04THE FINANCIAL
APPLICATIONPLANNED WORKLOAD

Learning to hedge.
Accounting for the cost.

The planned flagship benchmark tunes a small neural hedging policy on simulated price paths with transaction costs. Learning rate, network width, batch size, and regularization become the search space.

The study will assess optimization cost and independently evaluated hedging quality. Synthetic-market results will not be presented as evidence of live trading profitability.
